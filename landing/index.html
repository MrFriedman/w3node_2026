The problem

Files arrive incomplete, follow-up drags on, and riot claims stall on proof.

Conversations with Cape Town short-term brokerages on 13 November 2026. These are interview observations, not industry averages, HowlPay results, or service commitments.

Incomplete files still bounce.

One brokerage described about four rounds of follow-up per file, depending on severity and circumstance.

At another, submitting without a supporting document used to come back about a week later. A claim that should have been sorted in about two days was taking about three weeks a year or two ago. They are now around two days, because the file is not submitted until every required document is present. That figure is time to a complete, submitted file, not time to payment after approval.

Riot claims stall on proof of unrest.

One brokerage said riot claims need proof of political unrest in the area, and that this is the proof that gets queried or rejected most often.

Payment after approval still takes time.

One brokerage said it takes about five days to pay after approval, depending on the claim.

How it works

From a verified incident to a file the client can actually submit.

  1. Incident

    A verified, timestamped incident record is attached from an existing verified incident-alert feed for Cape Town small shops (partner).

  2. Pre-filled insurer form

    HowlPay prepares the insurer’s own claim form from the incident and the information already on file. It does not replace that form with a new portal.

  3. Checklist

    Missing fields and documents are listed for that cause of loss, before the file is sent.

  4. Client review and submission

    The client checks the pack and submits it themselves. The broker can help; in some offices they are not allowed to complete the form on the client’s behalf.

  5. Insurer approval

    A human at the insurer decides. The contract does not decide the claim. The legal policy still governs.

  6. Payout

    On the insurer or platform side, a USDT escrow can hold the payout and release it once the claim is approved. That rail is demonstrated on Sepolia testnet. It is not a broker feature, and a live production payout is still on the roadmap. Rand cash-out in the demo is a labelled simulation.

Who it’s for

Built to sit in the claims process, not to replace the people in it.

  • Underwriting managers and insurers

    Receive a complete file on your own form, with proof of unrest attached. HowlPay is meant to plug into an underwriting manager’s own broker app rather than compete with it. The escrow payout rail sits on this side of the market.

  • Embedded micro-insurers

    A claims process for spaza shops and other small businesses, with a testnet escrow that can hold and release a payout once a human approves.

  • Brokers

    A client-facing pre-fill, a missing-document checklist, and a familiar chat-style assistant with a handoff to someone at the brokerage. Not a new client portal. Escrow is not a broker product: many brokers never hold client money.

  • Shops

    A pack you can check and submit yourself. You do not have to hold USDT.

Validation evidence

What we heard on 13 November 2026.

Conversations with three Cape Town short-term brokerages and one underwriting manager. These are interview observations. Interest is not a paid pilot, and none of the figures below are HowlPay results.

  • Three brokerages said the insurer’s own claim form plus supporting documents is required. The pack cannot replace that form.
  • One brokerage expressed strong interest in auto-filling the insurer’s own forms from the policy and data already on file, with the client only signing.
  • A WhatsApp-style flow was familiar. A chatbot-only path was not: a live handoff to a person at the brokerage is required.
  • Data security, in line with legislation, was raised unprompted, and matters as much as speed.
  • Many brokers never hold client money. Debit orders go straight to the insurer, so escrow is not a broker product.
  • Two brokerages are open to reviewing a first version once their decision-makers have been briefed at the start of next week. That is still not a paid pilot.

Security and POPIA

The pack stays off-chain. Only its hash goes on-chain.

Names, photos, invoices, and other personal information are not written to the blockchain. The private claim pack is the record of what happened. The on-chain hash is an audit trail for that pack, not a copy of it.

A human insurer approves or rejects the claim. The legal policy still governs. The escrow demonstration runs on Sepolia testnet only.
